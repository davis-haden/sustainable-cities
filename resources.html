[ 01 — CAMPUS ]

Clean air is a campus issue

Georgia Tech is located in Midtown Atlanta, with parts of campus next to the Downtown Connector. Two of its dorms are so close to the highway, it inspired researchers to study how traffic pollution affects the people living in them (McKenzie). This indicates the significance of the air we breathe every day and how it presents itself as a real sustainability issue. Over the past two decades, researchers at Georgia Tech and Emory have linked Atlanta’s air quality to emergency room visits for asthma and cardiovascular disease (McKenzie), so clean air is a health issue as much as an environmental one.

[ 02 — SOURCES ]

Where it comes from

Traffic is the best-known source, but campus sources matter too. Open each one to see what Georgia Tech researchers and students have found.

  • When people stayed home during the 2020 COVID shutdown, Georgia Tech researchers measuring air from a rooftop station on the Ford ES&T building saw nitrogen dioxide levels fall as cars disappeared from the roads (“Professor Sally Ng’s Research”).

  • Students working on the air quality sensor project have noted that construction zones and buses are worth keeping an eye on (Langner and Abernathy).

  • Everyday activities like cooking and cleaning can cause spikes indoors (Langner and Abernathy).

[ 03 — MONITORING ]

How we track it

Georgia Tech students and researchers are measuring campus air quality in real time. A student team installed wireless sensors across campus, indoors and outdoors, with data available to anyone through the PurpleAir map (Langner and Abernathy).

[ 04 — EQUITY ]

Why it matters

The student team behind the sensor project lists equity as one of their main goals. Studies they cited showed how many low-income communities are exposed to more harmful air pollutants, with local data helping show where these problems are (Langner and Abernathy). The shutdown also showed that the air changes when our habits do. The data shows both how much progress is possible and how much there is left to do. Researchers and administrators can help but so can students. Students are part of this campus too, and what we do here counts. Go to the next tab to find out what you can do!
